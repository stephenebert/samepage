What failed
Case c09, Lumen youth exhibition, is a behaviorally equivalent pair. The two final deadlines differ in notation only: at millisecond precision, <=19:30:00.000 equals <19:30:00.001. The Spanish student-discount clause says "antes del" an explicit timestamp (before it). SamePage's Meta interpreter incorrectly requested clarification about whether that instant was included, returning null rules for Bob. Plain Meta correctly recognized equivalence. This is an unnecessary abstention, not a successfully detected ambiguity or an incomplete response. The original failure is retained unchanged.

What worked
Both approaches detected all six real outcome disagreements and gave independently valid concrete examples. SamePage generated seven witness examples across those six cases; plain review supplied one per case. All were valid. All 19 successfully compiled source policies matched the independent reference across 294,272 age/flag/time-partition outcomes. One of 20 resolved source policies was withheld, so this is not a 20/20 interpretation success.
Offline replay of the actual returned rules through the production state reducers verified nine pairs through separate simulated Alice/Bob approvals, disagreement holds, joint publication and 27 correct synthetic receipts. Two genuinely unresolved pairs correctly blocked approval. c09 also blocked, unnecessarily. These supplemental workflow checks are not a feature comparison against plain review and are not two-human usability testing.

Design and controls
Twelve new synthetic pairs were authored by one assistant and independently reviewed by another before calls: 6 different, 4 equivalent, 2 unresolved. No source corrections were needed. The holdout, scoring, prompts, order and production evaluator were hashed before the first request; 47 frozen files remain unchanged. Provider requests contained no goldens or reference attendees. Both arms used muse-spark-1.3, low reasoning, 8192 completion-token limits, isolated contexts, and balanced first-arm order. SamePage used its unchanged expression-v3 interpreter plus checker. Plain review received a competent prompt with the same domain and ambiguity requirements, without executable rules/tools. Source subject: 9d7b0c5755de3465610a592cfccd83bfd4d3ba7b. The separately gated benchmark transport was deployed in d465bbedb95316128d6ab542bca1922ec50fa577.

The server allowed one durable attempt per case/arm, 24 maximum, with no reset or retries. Whole-request byte bounds and output-token limits reserved a conservative $1.1406265 for the actual frozen requests, under the $2 run allowance. The normal Studio gate stayed disabled. This is an estimated run bound, not an account-wide spending limit.

Interpretation
There were 11 pairs both approaches classified correctly and one plain-review win. There were no SamePage classification wins. Plain review was also faster and cheaper in this run. This small synthetic, AI-authored/AI-reviewed benchmark does not establish general superiority, human usefulness, or hackathon winning odds. The policies deliberately contain known differences; these are not spontaneously generated model-repair mistakes.
The result does not justify claiming better review accuracy. SamePage's differentiated product behavior is converting agreed interpretation into checked rules, explicit approvals, publication and versioned form receipts. Its real-user value still needs validation.

Recommended next step
Address over-cautious temporal interpretation with general English/Spanish boundary semantics and regression coverage, preserving clarification for genuinely unclear wording. Retain this result unchanged and evaluate any revised version on a new frozen holdout. Do not keep rerunning this set until the score improves. For the demo, emphasize the concrete consequence, checked repair, two approvals and working form; do not claim this benchmark beat plain Meta.
